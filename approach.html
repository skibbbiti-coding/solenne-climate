02Approach

One studio from first sketch to the hundredth winter morning.

We are engineers, designers and installers under one roof. That lets us take responsibility for how a home actually feels — not just whether the equipment meets the drawings.

AThe process

Six phases, one team accountable for all of them.

Typical new residence: 14–24 months from first call to handover, running in step with the architect's own phases.

  1. 01

    Listening & load

    Schematic design · 2–4 weeks

    We walk the site, read the orientation and the microclimate — marine layer, canyon winds, afternoon sun on west glass — and sit down with the owners to understand how they actually live. Then we run a room-by-room Manual J on the schematic set and share it with the architect while changes are still cheap.

    • Comfort brief
    • Manual J load study
    • Emitter strategy
    • Mechanical room footprint
  2. 02

    Coordinated design

    Design development · 6–10 weeks

    We design the whole system together: heat source, buffer and distribution, radiant zones, ventilation paths, dehumidification and controls. Our drawings land in the architect's set as clean backgrounds — slab build-ups, ceiling depths, slot diffuser locations and every penetration through the structure.

    • Hydronic schematic
    • Radiant layout by zone
    • Ventilation & duct routing
    • Acoustic review
  3. 03

    Title 24, permits & proposal

    Construction documents · 4–6 weeks

    We prepare the Title 24 energy compliance, select equipment against both the load and the noise criterion, and issue a fixed design-build proposal with clear allowances. No change orders hiding in the details — the design is ours, so the risk is too.

    • Title 24 compliance
    • Equipment schedule
    • Fixed proposal
    • Permit set
  4. 04

    Rough-in & slab

    Construction · in step with the GC

    Our own crews — never subcontracted — lay radiant tubing, run ventilation and set the mechanical room. Every loop is pressure-tested before and during the pour, photographed, and mapped onto the as-builts so future trades know exactly where not to drill.

    • Pre-pour pressure test
    • Photo-mapped as-builts
    • Weekly site reports
  5. 05

    Commissioning

    Handover · 2–3 weeks

    We balance every loop and air path, verify dew-point logic, and measure sound in each bedroom with the system at design load. Then we hand over a plain-language manual for the owners, a technical binder for the house manager and remote monitoring for us.

    • Flow & air balancing
    • Sound measurements
    • Owner's manual
    • Monitoring set-up
  6. 06

    Stewardship

    Ongoing

    A radiant home settles over its first year as the slab dries and the seasons turn. We tune the curves each season, replace filters, watch the data remotely and assign a named technician who knows the house — and the owners — by name.

    • Seasonal tuning
    • Remote monitoring
    • Priority service line
BSystems, explained

What we install, in your terms.

Radiant floors

Water at little more than body temperature runs through tubing under your floor. The whole surface becomes a gentle source of warmth — or coolness in summer — so there are no drafts, no hot and cold spots, and nothing to see.

PEX at 6–9" o.c. in a 1.5–4" topping slab or thin-set panels for timber floors. Plan for 1.5–2" of build-up over structure, R-10 under-slab insulation, and avoid large rugs over sunlit zones. Stone, concrete and engineered oak all perform well.

Typical specSupply 85–110°F heating, 60–65°F cooling

Radiant heating & cooling service

Radiant ceilings

Thin panels hidden behind a plaster ceiling cool the room the way a stone church stays cool in July — by quietly absorbing heat from you and the room, rather than blowing cold air at you. Ideal for bedrooms.

Gypsum or lime-plaster-finished capillary or aluminium-plate panels, 1–2" deep. Needs a 4–6" service cavity for headers and coordination with lighting layouts; we provide reflected ceiling plan overlays showing panel-free zones.

Typical specUp to 25 Btu/h·ft² sensible cooling

Radiant heating & cooling service

Air-to-water heat pumps

An all-electric outdoor unit that makes warm or chilled water for the floors, ceilings and showers — three to four times more efficiently than a gas boiler. We place it far from bedrooms and screen it within the landscape.

Monobloc units, typically 3–5 tons each, staged in pairs for redundancy. Allow a 4 × 10 ft pad per pair with 3 ft service clearance, an acoustic enclosure line in the landscape set, and a 120-gal buffer in the mechanical room.

Typical specSeasonal COP 3.2–4.1 on coastal climate data

Air-to-water heat pump service

Energy recovery ventilation

A tight, efficient home needs fresh air on purpose. An ERV brings in filtered outdoor air and recovers most of the heat and moisture from the stale air it exhausts — so the house always smells fresh without wasting energy.

Balanced supply and exhaust at 0.3–0.4 ACH, MERV-13 filtration, supply through linear slots in millwork or ceiling reveals. Allow a 24 × 48" unit in a conditioned plenum and two exterior hoods spaced 10 ft apart.

Typical spec75–82% sensible recovery · <20 dBA at diffuser

Dehumidification & dew point

Coastal summers are humid more often than they are hot. Keeping humidity near 50% is what makes a 74° room feel crisp instead of clammy — and it's what lets radiant cooling run safely, with no condensation, ever.

Ducted whole-house dehumidifier sharing the ERV supply path; per-zone dew-point sensors interlocked with chilled-water mixing. Coordinate a condensate route and a 30 × 30" service access in the plenum.

Typical specSurface temp held ≥ 2°F above dew point

Controls & solar thermal

Small, discreet sensors in each room and a single app that's actually pleasant to use. Where the roof allows, solar thermal panels pre-heat pool and domestic water for free on most days of the year.

Flush-mount sensors that accept the electrical trim of your choice; outdoor-reset curves with zone-level dew-point override. Solar thermal needs ~60 ft² of south-facing roof per 1,000 gal of pool — we coordinate layout and screening.

Typical specRoom sensors ± 0.2°F, open-protocol controller

Ongoing care
CQuestions

Asked often, answered plainly.

When should we bring you into a project?

Ideally during schematic design, once there's a plan and sections but before structure and ceiling heights are locked. Radiant systems live in the slab and ceilings, so early coordination is what keeps them invisible — and avoids the soffits and grilles that appear when HVAC is added late.

Can radiant floors really cool a house?

Yes — with the right design. Radiant cooling handles the sensible load (the heat you feel) very effectively on the Central Coast, where peak loads are moderate. We pair it with an ERV and dehumidifier to handle moisture, and controls that keep every surface safely above the dew point.

For very glassy west-facing rooms we sometimes add a small, concealed fan coil for peak afternoons. We'll tell you at the load-study stage if that's needed.

Isn't radiant slow to respond?

A thick slab is slow to change, which is exactly why it's so stable. We control it with outdoor-reset and weather-forecast logic so it anticipates rather than reacts. For rooms used intermittently — guest suites, studios — we specify low-mass panels or radiant ceilings that respond within the hour.

How quiet is "quiet"?

We design bedrooms to NC-20 and living spaces to NC-25 — roughly the background level of a recording studio. Because radiant surfaces carry the heating and cooling, airflow is a small fraction of a conventional system, and there are no air handlers cycling on and off. We measure at handover and put the numbers in writing.

Can you convert an existing home to all-electric?

Often, yes. About a third of our work is retrofits of older Riviera, Montecito and Ojai homes. Thin-profile radiant panels can go under new floors without raising them more than an inch, and air-to-water heat pumps can replace an old boiler using existing radiators if they're sized for lower water temperatures. We start with a site survey and a frank feasibility report.

How do you handle Title 24 and permitting?

In-house. We model the building, prepare the compliance documentation, coordinate HERS verification and manage the mechanical permit. Because we designed the system, we can usually find efficiency credits the architect can trade for more glass or a simpler envelope detail.

Who works on our house?

Our own employed crews — we don't subcontract installation. NATE-certified technicians handle commissioning and service, and each home is assigned a named project lead and, after handover, a named service technician.

What does ongoing care involve?

Two seasonal visits a year for filters, water chemistry and curve tuning, plus remote monitoring that flags anything unusual before you'd notice it. Stewardship clients get a direct priority line and same-day response within our service region.
