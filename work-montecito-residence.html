Olive Grove Residence

A 7,400 sq ft single-level home among century-old olive trees, heated and cooled entirely through its limestone floors and plaster ceilings. No grilles, no condensers in view, no gas.

Fig. 01 South elevation. The heat pumps sit 40 feet away inside a planted, louvred enclosure designed with the landscape architect.

01The brief

A house that should feel like the shade of an olive tree.

The owners came to Ledger & Vane with one sentence: they wanted the house to feel the way it feels under the old trees on the site at four in the afternoon. Still air, cool stone, no hum. The architects called us during schematic design, before a single ceiling height was fixed.

The plan is long and low, with a 140-foot glazed gallery facing south. Glass that generous usually means a forced-air system working hard at the perimeter. Our job was to make that glazing comfortable without anything appearing on the walls or the ceiling plane.

Our first deliverable was a room-by-room Manual J on the schematic set — it moved two skylights and added an exterior shade, cutting the gallery's peak cooling load by 31% before we chose equipment.
02Design

Designing around the slab.

Heating and cooling run through PEX tubing at 6" on centre in a 4" topping slab beneath the limestone, split into fourteen zones so the sunlit gallery and the shaded bedroom wing can be treated differently on the same afternoon. In the bedrooms, where the owners wanted cooler nights, we added radiant ceiling panels finished in lime plaster.

Radiant cooling only works if the surfaces stay above the dew point. A ducted dehumidifier and two energy recovery ventilators keep indoor humidity near 50%, and the controls watch dew point room by room — throttling chilled water a degree before condensation could ever become possible.

Supply air is ventilation only — around 0.35 air changes per hour — delivered through 3/4" linear slots in the millwork reveals the architects had already drawn.

“The best compliment we received at handover was that nobody could find the system.”

Solenne project lead, on commissioning day
03Build

A mechanical room as considered as the kitchen.

Our crews set every loop before the pour and held the system at 100 psi for 24 hours while the concrete went in. The mechanical room was drawn to the same standard as the house: the manifold is labelled by room name, piping runs on a single level, and every valve is reachable without a ladder.

Outside, the two heat pumps sit behind a louvred cedar enclosure 40 feet from the nearest bedroom, on isolation pads, with low-speed fans selected specifically for night operation.

Measured at the property line at 2 a.m. in August: 31 dBA — quieter than the olive leaves.
