01How it works

Comfort from surfaces, not moving air.

Your body loses most of its heat by radiation to the surfaces around it. Change the temperature of the floor or ceiling by a few degrees and the whole room feels different — without a fan, a register or a breath of draft. That is why a radiant home can feel warmer at 68°F than a forced-air home at 72°F.

We run PEX tubing in the slab, in thin-profile panels under timber floors, or behind plaster ceilings, fed by a manifold in a mechanical room we design as carefully as the kitchen. In summer the same loops carry cool water, quietly absorbing heat from the room while a small, separate ventilation system keeps humidity in check.

02What's included

Designed, installed and commissioned in-house.

  • Room-by-room Manual J on the architect's drawings, with surface-temperature limits checked for every floor finish.
  • Radiant layout by zone — slab, thin-set panel or plaster ceiling, chosen per room for response time and finish.
  • Dew-point control for cooling: humidity sensing in each cooled zone, interlocked with chilled-water mixing.
  • Pre-pour pressure test held through the concrete pour, photographed and mapped onto as-builts.
  • Balancing and sound measurement in every bedroom before handover, with the numbers in writing.
03For architects

What we need on your drawings.

Plan for 1.5–2" of topping or panel build-up over structure and R-10 under-slab insulation on grade. Radiant ceilings need a 4–6" service cavity; we provide reflected-ceiling overlays showing panel-free zones for lighting and sprinklers. One mechanical room of roughly 80–120 sq ft serves most residences up to 8,000 sq ft.

04Questions

Radiant, answered plainly.

Can radiant floors cool a house on the Central Coast?

Yes. Coastal peak cooling loads are moderate, which suits radiant cooling well. We pair it with an ERV and dehumidifier so surfaces always stay above the dew point, and add a small concealed fan coil only where a very glassy west-facing room needs help on the hottest afternoons.

Which floor finishes work with radiant?

Stone, tile, polished concrete and engineered timber all perform well. Solid wide-plank hardwood and thick rugs are possible with care — we check every finish's surface-temperature limit during design.

Can you add radiant to an existing home?

Often. Thin-profile panels add about an inch under a new floor, and radiant ceiling panels can go in during a ceiling replacement. We start with a site survey and an honest feasibility note.

How long does radiant tubing last?

Oxygen-barrier PEX embedded in concrete is designed for a service life measured in decades. The parts that need attention — pumps, valves, sensors — live in the mechanical room where they are easy to reach, and are looked after under the Care Plan.
